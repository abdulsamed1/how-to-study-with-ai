# Phase 7 — Software design and test strategy



**Goal:** Make changes safe to reason about and cheap to verify. Agent-written code and tests are review inputs: write the acceptance criteria first, then verify independently (rule 10).

**Handbook reading path (S69 [TOC]):** Ch. 21 §§21.1–21.3, 21.5, 21.7, 21.10–21.19 (test types, test doubles, real-PostgreSQL integration, provider boundaries, LLM tests, contracts, TDD, mutation/performance testing, and CI); Ch. 22 §§22.1–22.11, 22.16 (Git, merge conflicts, PRs, review, leaked secrets); Ch. 23 §§23.1–23.5, 23.10–23.14 (maintainable code, refactoring, backend patterns, error handling); Ch. 28 §§28.2–28.6, 28.14–28.16 (quality trade-offs, boundaries, ports/adapters, ADRs). Skip framework-specific Jest/NestJS/Supertest examples unless the conditional P7.7/P7.8 or a named role makes that code necessary.

- **P7.1 — Boundaries, responsibilities, and dependency direction** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P2.5, P3.1, P3.2, P3.4–P3.7, P4.7a. Separate transport, application use cases, domain rules, and persistence where useful. **Proof:** draw a module diagram, justify each boundary, and add an automated check (script or lint rule) that fails on a deliberate dependency-direction violation. Sources: S20 ch. 2–3 and 8 [TOC], S26 ch. 2–3 (read), S38 ch. 2–5 [TOC].
- **P7.2 — Unit, integration, contract, and end-to-end tests** — **Priority:** must-have. Depends on: P2.2, P4.7a, P5.6. Choose the test level that proves a claim; avoid testing implementation details unnecessarily. **Proof:** test one important invariant at multiple appropriate layers, with at least one test designed from the requirement before reading the implementation and shown to fail when a defect is deliberately seeded. Sources: F8, S20, S40, S41.
- **P7.3 — Test doubles and dependency boundaries** — **Priority:** must-have. Depends on: P7.1–P7.2. Know when a fake, stub, or real dependency improves confidence and when it hides a defect. **Proof:** replace one brittle test without losing meaningful integration coverage; review one agent-generated test for assertions that only restate the implementation or mock the dependency under test, and record what it would fail to catch. **AI-dependency bridge:** seed one defect in the provider adapter that a mock-coupled suite still passes, then write the independent test that fails under the defect and passes after the fix. Sources: F8, S41.
- **P7.4 — Refactoring under tests and code review** — **Priority:** must-have. Depends on: P7.1–P7.3, P2.1, P2.2. Change structure without silently changing behavior. **Proof:** a reviewed refactor with a green regression suite and explanatory diff, plus an agent review record (§3) for one actual diff produced by an AI coding tool such as OpenCode: the diff is small enough to review; the files, dependencies, and side effects it touches are listed and the key changes explained unaided; at least one finding is confirmed by a failing test or runtime evidence and at least one suspected finding is rejected or revised after checking evidence; verification does not rely on the agent's own tests; a regression suite exists; and the accept, reject, or redirect decision is recorded. The learner can explain and modify the result unaided (`../../AGENTS.md` §11). Sources: S34, S37, S40, S41; S26 ch. 7–9 [TOC].
- **P7.5a — Architecture characteristics, quality scenarios, styles, and decomposition** — Implementation unit (multi-session). **Priority:** important. Depends on: P7.1–P7.4. Learn to move from a real problem to an architecture that fits its constraints, rather than defaulting to a familiar style. The unit covers four connected skills:

  1. **Architecture characteristics:** identify which quality attributes matter for the actual problem (for example, availability, latency, modifiability, security, deployability, cost), which are hard constraints, and which compete with one another. Source-backed: S20 ch. 4–5 define, identify, and scope architecture characteristics; S19 ch. 2 defines nonfunctional requirements with measurable targets.
  2. **Measurable quality scenarios:** turn each important quality attribute into an observable scenario with a stimulus, expected response, threshold, and measurement method. Avoid vague criteria such as "high performance" or "reliable." Source-backed: S19 ch. 2 (latency percentiles, availability targets, throughput, growth); S42 (C4 model — review checklist helps verify diagram quality, not quality attributes themselves).
  3. **Architecture styles and boundaries:** understand the relevant trade-offs among a simple monolith, modular monolith, layered architecture, event-driven architecture, service-based architecture, and separately deployed services. The question is always which style satisfies the identified characteristics at the lowest justified complexity. Source-backed: S20 ch. 9 [TOC] and 11 (architecture foundations, modular monolith); S20 ch. 19 (choosing appropriate style); S26 ch. 2–3 (read; modeling and splitting the monolith); S33 (workload segmentation — vendor guidance: compare with the books before adopting).
  4. **Coupling and decomposition:** assess responsibilities, dependency direction (building on P7.1), data ownership, change patterns, operational coupling, and deployment boundaries before splitting components or services. Source-backed: S38 ch. 2–5 and 7 [TOC] (coupling, modularity, decomposition, service granularity); S26 ch. 2–3 (read).

  **Proof:** a quality-attribute table for a realistic scenario, with at least three attributes that have measurable targets and named measurement methods; and a written comparison of at least two plausible architecture styles (including the simplest adequate option) against the identified attributes, with explicit trade-offs. Sources: S20 ch. 4–6, 11, and 19 (read) and ch. 7, 9 [TOC]; S26 ch. 2–3; S38 ch. 2–5 and 7 [TOC]; S19 ch. 1–2; S33; S41.

- **P7.5b — Architecture diagrams and architectural decision records** — Implementation unit (multi-session). **Priority:** important. Depends on: P7.5a. Record an architecture decision so it can be checked, communicated, and revisited, before any AI coding agent implements it. The unit covers two connected skills:

  1. **Architecture diagrams:** select the appropriate level of detail and communicate system context, components, dependencies, and important runtime interactions without creating misleading diagrams. Use the C4 model's abstraction hierarchy (system context → container → component) as a practical default; other notations are acceptable when they serve the same purpose. Source-backed: S20 ch. 23 (diagramming architecture); S42 (C4 model: abstractions, diagrams, notation, review checklist).
  2. **Decision records:** record the context, requirements identified in P7.5a, plausible alternatives, constraints, rationale, consequences, risks, and conditions for revisiting a decision. The ADR must be written before any AI coding agent implements the chosen design. Source-backed: S20 ch. 21 (architectural decisions); S38 ch. 1 and 15 [TOC] (trade-off analysis and ADRs).

  The architecture decision exercise must compare at least three plausible options, including the simplest adequate option and a meaningful comparison between an in-process modular boundary and a separately deployed alternative. Quality attributes must have measurable targets from P7.5a. Constraints, consequences, unresolved risks, and conditions that would trigger reconsideration are recorded. This ADR is the one later design units reuse — revisited in P10.4a–P10.4c, which extend it rather than producing a second three-option exercise.

  **Proof:** an ADR written before any agent implements the chosen option, comparing at least three options (including simplest adequate and modular-monolith vs. separately deployed), plus appropriately scoped architecture diagrams (at minimum a context diagram and a component/container diagram). Compare any vendor guidance used (S33) with the books before adopting it. Sources: S20 ch. 19, 21, 23; S38 ch. 1 and 15 [TOC]; S42; S33; S41.

- **P7.5c — Fitness functions and architecture risk analysis** — Implementation unit (multi-session). **Priority:** important. Depends on: P7.5a, P7.5b. Validate the decision recorded in P7.5b so its most important constraints stay observable and its risks are honest. The unit covers two connected skills:

  1. **Fitness functions and governance:** show how a valuable architectural constraint can be checked automatically or monitored consistently — for example, a dependency-direction check (extending P7.1's proof), a response-time budget in CI, or a module-coupling metric. The point is not to automate everything but to make the most important constraints observable. Source-backed: S20 ch. 6 (measuring and governing architecture characteristics, fitness functions).
  2. **Architecture risk analysis:** identify risky assumptions, expensive-to-reverse decisions, unknown failure modes, and the cheapest practical way to validate them before committing. Source-backed: S20 ch. 22 (analyzing architecture risk).

  **Proof:** at least one architecture fitness check that can be validated (automated, or a named manual verification method), plus a risk assessment listing the top assumptions from the P7.5b ADR, their potential impact, and the cheapest way to test each. Sources: S20 ch. 6 and 22; S41.

- **P7.6 — Evolutionary architecture decisions: change cost and build-vs-buy** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P7.5b, P7.5c. Treat the architecture as evolutionary: identify which decisions are expensive to reverse (the P7.5b ADR consequences) and schedule their re-evaluation against the P7.5c fitness and risk hooks. Then build-vs-buy: for each candidate capability — build, buy, or adopt open source — weigh fit to the recorded requirements, multi-year ownership cost (operations, upgrades, exit), lock-in, and differentiation. **Proof:** the P7.5b ADR extended with one explicit evolutionary decision (what will change, the trigger condition that forces re-evaluation), plus one build-vs-buy comparison whose costs state their assumptions. Revisit triggers are re-used by the design review, re-tested by P10.7. Sources: S26 ch. 16 (read); S38 ch. 15 [TOC]; S33.

- **P7.7 — Next.js server/client boundary and data flow** — Implementation unit (multi-session). **Priority:** important. Depends on: P2.3, P3.1, P4.3, P4.7a, P7.1. Scope: the section 2 Next.js target, not a Next.js track. Read the existing website across the server/client boundary: which components render on the server, which run on the client, where data is fetched and cached, how streaming and navigation change what is re-fetched, and how cancellation and races are handled. Use opran's website and ADR-001, ADR-002, ADR-004, ADR-005, ADR-007, and ADR-013 as the worked system; treat the ADRs as docs-only until each claim is checked against the file. Do not re-teach HTTP (P4.2–P4.3) or React basics. **Proof:** a one-page data-flow map of one opran route that labels server-rendered, cached, client-fetched, and streamed data; for each boundary name the ADR that chose it, one failure mode (stale cache, request waterfall, hydration mismatch, or an uncancelled race), and one check that would confirm it; then defend two of the decisions without an agent. Sources: S47; opran `website/` and ADR-001, ADR-002, ADR-004, ADR-005, ADR-007, ADR-013 (docs-only until verified in code).
- **P7.8 — Twenty's NestJS host and Apps extension model** — Implementation unit (multi-session). **Priority:** important. Depends on: P3.4, P3.7, P4.4, P7.1, P7.5b. Scope: platform literacy at the section 2 NestJS target, explicitly not a NestJS track. Separate what the Twenty host provides from what the Eagles app defines: the server and worker processes, NestJS modules/providers/dependency injection at the level needed to read the host, workspace objects/fields/roles, logic functions, front components, and the core-patch boundary (AD-31). Use AD-32 and AD-33 to explain platform limits without claiming knowledge of Twenty internals the learner has not read. Do not re-teach Node process basics (P3.4–P3.7), authentication (P4.4), or domain boundaries (P7.1). **Proof:** a host-versus-app boundary map for Eagles that names, for each layer, one thing the platform provides, one thing the app owns, one decision (AD-31, AD-32, or AD-33), and what would break if the boundary were crossed; verify one logic function's use of the host contract from `eagles-wrms-app/src/application-config.ts` and one function file, and mark anything not checked `docs-only`. Sources: S45, S46; Eagles AD-31–AD-33 and `eagles-wrms-app/src/application-config.ts` (verified); `src/logic-functions/` (verified file list).
