# Phase 10 — FDE discovery, solution selection, and system design

**Goal:** Choose the right solution before building it. Apply the database, correctness, software architecture, distributed-systems, and production knowledge already acquired; do not re-teach those mechanisms inside system-design exercises.

**Handbook reading path (S69 [TOC]):** Ch. 28–30 as mapped below. Read only the case studies matching the selected workflow; sample numbers are not measured facts about the capstone.

- **P10.1 — Workflow observation and stakeholder questions** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P2.1, P7.1. Map inputs, decisions, handoffs, exceptions, and existing tools. **Proof:** current-state workflow map, stakeholder questions, exception paths, baseline metrics, constraints, and initial AI justification; simulated discovery must be labelled. Sources: delivery loop; current discovery-practice source gap remains open in resources.
- **P10.2 — Deterministic code vs workflow vs agent** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.1, P7.5b, P8.1. Choose the least complex design that meets the need; identify where autonomy is useful and dangerous. This is a provisional complexity floor revisited in P11.6 with model evidence. **Proof:** option comparison including a non-AI option. Sources: S3, S12.
- **P10.3 — Baseline, outcome metric, and adoption constraints** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P2.1, P9.4, P10.1. Separate technical proxy metrics from business outcomes and plan collection. **Proof:** measurement plan with owner and collection method. Sources: delivery loop; outcome-measurement source gap remains open in resources.
- **P10.4a — System design I: problem, requirements, and capacity (Stages 1–3)** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.1–P10.3, P7.5a, P9.6. Frame workflow and stakeholders; separate functional/nonfunctional requirements; choose quality attributes and measurable targets; state workload assumptions; estimate capacity with units and uncertainty. **Proof:** design-brief part 1 with problem, requirements, workload assumptions, and capacity worksheet. Sources: S19 ch. 1–2; S20 ch. 4–5; S28 ch. 1 (read).
- **P10.5 — Read-path and overload mechanisms: decisions, not formulas** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P9.6, P9.8–P9.9, P10.2. Decide and justify caching/read replicas, invalidation and freshness, rate limiting, load shedding, queue-based load leveling, statelessness, and load balancing. Diagnose in P9.6; select and justify here. Use the performance journey for deeper diagnostic techniques. **Proof:** include/defer/reject decision per mechanism, tied to a P10.4a requirement and named failure (cache loss, hot tenant, or overload). Sources: S25; S27 ch. 28 (read); S28 ch. 6 (read); S29 ch. 16 (read); S19 ch. 6 (read).
- **P10.6 — Distribution trade-offs: consistency, partitioning, and coordination** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P8.0c–P8.0e, P10.5. Decide which paths need which consistency level, what should remain single-node, where partitioning/sharding is justified, how rebalancing changes the cost, and when coordination is necessary or avoidable. Reuse the mechanisms from Phase 8; do not teach consensus or replication from scratch here. **Proof:** trade-off record for one intended distribution, with per-path consistency, partition key, coordinator/leader assumptions, and failure consequences. Sources: S19 ch. 7 and 10 (read); S29 ch. 2 (read); S28 ch. 1 [TOC]; S18 course pointer only.
- **P10.4b — System design II: interfaces, data, components, and architecture decision (Stages 4–6)** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P10.5–P10.6, P4.6, P5.6, P6.6, P7.5b, P7.9–P7.10, P8.7. Design API/integration contracts, data models, invariants, ownership, read/write paths, component responsibilities, trust boundaries, and sync/async decisions. Reuse the P7.5b ADR and extend it with one new decision specific to this design. **Proof:** design-brief part 2 with API/data-ownership boundaries, context/component diagrams, and ADR alternatives/consequences. Sources: S20 ch. 8, 11, 19 (read); S26 ch. 4–5 (selected sections read); S38 ch. 6, 9–10, 13 [TOC/selected sections].
- **P10.4c — System design III: failure, operations, and validation (Stages 7–9)** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4b, P9.4, P9.7–P9.11, P7.5c. Analyze reliability, partial failure, overload, data integrity, access control, secrets, degradation, observability, deployment, rollback, and cost. Define validation before implementation: tests, workload experiments, fault scenarios, thresholds, rollout, and recovery criteria. Execution of AI-specific evaluation remains in Phase 14. **Proof:** design-brief part 3 with failure/security matrix, named owners/observability paths, and validation plan. Sources: S27 ch. 23–28 (selected sections/TOC); S29 ch. 14 and 16 (read); S24; S25.
- **P10.8 — Design exercises: rate limiter, notification service, and ledger/payment flow** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P10.5–P10.6. Rehearse the design method in three distinct workloads. **Proof:** one exercise packet with capacity assumptions, failure matrix, and map of reused units for each scenario. Sources: S25; S27 ch. 28 (read); S19 ch. 7 (read).
- **P10.7 — Design review before implementation: go/adjust/stop** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a–P10.4c, P10.3. Adversarially review the brief against the Problem Card, cost assumptions, weaknesses, unvalidated risks, and explicit v1 exclusions. **Proof:** review record with at least three concrete weaknesses and a go/adjust/stop decision with reasons. Sources: S20 ch. 22; S33.
- **P10.9 — Analytical data architecture and data products** — **Priority:** useful. Depends on: P5.8, P8.9–P8.10, P10.4b. Use only when the workflow needs reporting, analytics, or shared data products. Compare OLTP and analytical workloads, ETL/ELT, warehouse/lake/lakehouse patterns, operational data integration, data ownership, and Data Mesh as an organizational/architectural choice rather than a mandatory platform. **Proof:** data-flow and ownership decision record for one analytical use case, including freshness, privacy, lineage, failure handling, and why a simpler reporting path is or is not adequate. Sources: S19 ch. 1, 3, 11–12 (selected sections read); S38 ch. 14 [TOC].
