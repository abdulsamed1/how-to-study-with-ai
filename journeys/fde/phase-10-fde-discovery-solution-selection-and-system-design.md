# Phase 10 — FDE discovery, solution selection, and system design



**Goal:** Choose the right solution before building it. This phase reuses the engineering knowledge already acquired.

**Handbook reading path (S69 [TOC]):** Ch. 28 §§28.2–28.10, 28.12–28.19 (quality attributes, architecture options, boundaries, event-driven design, multi-tenancy, AI-enabled products, ADRs, technical debt); Ch. 29 §§29.2–29.11 (requirements, estimates, API/data model, scaling/availability, consistency, retention, cost, explicit trade-offs); Ch. 30 §30.2 (rate limiter), §30.3 (notifications), §30.8 (RAG), §§30.10–30.12 (checkout/inventory, scheduler, event ingestion). Choose only the case studies matching the selected workflow; their numbers are examples, not measured facts about the capstone.

- **P10.1 — Workflow observation and stakeholder questions** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P2.1, P7.1. Map inputs, decisions, handoffs, exceptions, and existing tools. **Proof:** current-state workflow map, stakeholder interview questions, exception paths, baseline metrics, constraints, and initial AI justification; if conducted with a simulated stakeholder, label the exercise explicitly as a simulated discovery. Sources: [delivery loop](../../teaching/delivery-loop.md); external discovery-practice sources remain NO-EVIDENCE — see `../../workspace/resources.md` coverage gaps.
- **P10.2 — Deterministic code vs workflow vs agent** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.1, P7.5b, P8.1. Role split: this comparison is the **provisional complexity floor** — it does not require model knowledge and can only reject AI before it is justified; the comparison is revisited in P11.6 with model-capability evidence, and pattern mechanics are revisited in P13.1. Choose the least complex design that meets the need; identify where autonomy is useful and where it is dangerous; label each option at the evidence stage it can reach (demo, working system, deployed outcome; [delivery loop](../../teaching/delivery-loop.md)). **Proof:** written option comparison including a non-AI option, marked provisional against the P11.6 revisit. Sources: S3 (note its banner: tooling landscape changed since Dec 2024 — patterns stand, tooling examples need re-check), S12.
- **P10.3 — Baseline, outcome metric, and adoption constraints** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P2.1, P9.4, P10.1. Role split: P2.1 declared the baseline hypothesis in the Problem Card; this unit plans its collection — separate technical proxy metrics from business outcomes and produce the measurement plan. The outcome report is P16.5; do not duplicate it here. **Proof:** measurement plan with owner and collection method. Sources: [delivery loop](../../teaching/delivery-loop.md); external measurement-planning sources remain NO-EVIDENCE — see `../../workspace/resources.md` coverage gaps.
- **P10.4a — System design I: frame the problem, requirements, and capacity (Stages 1–3)** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.1, P10.2, P10.3, P7.5a, P9.6. Implementation unit (multi-session): the first part of the system-design brief. Stage 1 frames the real workflow, stakeholders, inputs, existing systems, and unresolved requirements. Stage 2 separates functional from nonfunctional requirements, picks quality attributes (P7.5a) with measurable acceptance targets, and records workload assumptions (request rate, concurrency, bursts, read/write mix, retention, growth). Stage 3 runs transparent back-of-the-envelope capacity estimates with assumptions, units, and uncertainty; no manufactured precision. Boundary: this unit chooses estimated numbers; the performance map (L6, L7) owns diagnosing realized load later — do not duplicate its diagnosis method. Source-backed: S19 ch. 2, S20 ch. 4–5 (read); S28 ch. 1 (read: scalability dimensions, scale up vs scale out, the two scaling principles, the cost of capacity), ch. 2 [TOC]; S19 ch. 1 (data-system trade-offs). **Proof:** brief part 1 — problem statement with constraints, measurable requirements, workload assumptions, and a capacity-estimation worksheet.

- **P10.5 — Read-path and overload mechanisms: decisions, not formulas** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P9.6, P10.2. Decide and justify the mechanisms that protect and scale read paths for the capstone: caching and read replicas (staleness, invalidation, and the cache-failure-collapse criterion: a cache outage must not turn into origin overload), rate limiting (quota keys, shared limits, hot-tenant isolation, returning 429 with `Retry-After` server-side), load shedding and queue-based load leveling, statelessness, and the load-balancing decision surface. Diagnosis stays in P9.6; the performance map (`../performance/study-overview.md` L3.6, L5, L6.3–L6.4) supplies criteria and failure modes — this unit owns the choice and justification. **Proof:** a written include/defer/reject decision per mechanism, each tied to a P10.4a requirement and a named failure scenario (notably cache-vanish and one-tenant-dominates). Sources: S25; S27 ch. 28 (read: load shedding, load leveling, rate limiting and its coordination cost); S28 ch. 6 (read: caching, TTL and invalidation, cache-aside resilience); S29 ch. 16 (read: retry amplification, throttling, timeouts and triage); S19 ch. 6 (read: replication lag). Mentor synthesis remains for the cache-collapse criterion, which comes from the performance map (L3.6, L5).

- **P10.6 — Distribution trade-offs: consistency, partitioning, and coordination as design decisions** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P10.5. Scale-up vs scale-out, bottleneck migration that motivates scale-out, "the database follows the application", and statelessness as the horizontal-scaling precondition. Then the distributed-data decisions a design must make consciously: which consistency level each access path needs (and when it is negotiable), partitioning keys and rebalancing cost, and where coordination — locking, distributed transactions, consensus — is genuinely required versus avoided. The lesson defines the consistency vocabulary (strong, eventual, quorum) before asking for a per-path choice. No mechanism teaching here; deep background stays in S18/S19 readings. **Proof:** a trade-off record for one intended distribution: what stays single-node, what partitions, the consistency per path, and what breaks under the P10.4a workload assumptions. Sources: S19 ch. 10 (read: eventual vs strong consistency, linearizability, consensus), ch. 7 (read: sharding and when to avoid it); S29 ch. 2 (read: strong vs eventual consistency, the cost of coordination, CAP); S28 ch. 1 (read: scaling up vs out, consistency with replication); S18 (MIT 6.5840 course materials — pointer only, lecture notes not opened); perf map L6.1–L6.2, L6.6.

- **P10.4b — System design II: interfaces, data, components, and the architecture decision (Stages 4–6)** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P10.5–P10.6, P4.6, P5.6, P6.6, P7.5b, P8.7. Implementation unit (multi-session). Stage 4 designs API and integration contracts, data models, invariants, ownership, read/write paths — reusing P4.3–P4.6, P5.1–P5.6, P6.1–P6.6, P8.2 rather than restating them. Stage 5 assigns component responsibilities, dependency direction, trust boundaries, state ownership, and the sync/async decision. Stage 6 compares alternatives and justifies complexity: the P7.5b ADR is reused and extended with exactly one new decision specific to this design — never a second three-option exercise. Mechanism knowledge for the comparison comes from P10.5 and P10.6. If Stages 4–6 stall, deliver a minimum-viable brief: one interface boundary, one data owner, one component decision. Source-backed: S20 ch. 8 (read: component-based thinking, logical vs physical architecture), ch. 9 [TOC], 11, 19 (read); S26 ch. 4 (read: communication styles, inter-process failure modes), ch. 5 [TOC]; S29 ch. 2 (read: APIs and sync/async calls, idempotency, delivery semantics, consistency); S38 ch. 6, 9–10, 15 [TOC] (no epub locally — read in the study tool); S42. **Proof:** brief part 2 — interface and data-ownership boundaries, context + component diagrams, and the ADR (P7.5b's, plus one new P10.4b-specific decision with alternatives and consequences).

- **P10.4c — System design III: failure, operations, and validation (Stages 7–9)** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4b, P9.4, P9.7, P7.5c. Coupled with: P14.1–P14.3, P15.1. Implementation unit (multi-session), finishing the brief. Stage 7 analyzes reliability, failure, and security — partial failures, overload, data integrity, access control, secrets, degradation — reusing P8 and P9, and the Phase 15 security work revisited in P15.1–P15.6, rather than restating; each important failure gets an owner and an observability path. Stage 8 plans operations, observability, deployment, rollback, and cost, tied to a business outcome and owner (reuses P9.1–P9.7). Stage 9 defines validation before implementation: tests, workload experiments, fault scenarios, thresholds, rollout and recovery criteria; state unresolved assumptions and the cheapest experiment that could invalidate the design. Its execution lives in Phase 14 — this unit plans it, not runs it. Source-backed: S27 ch. 28 (read: load shedding, load leveling, rate limiting), ch. 24–27 [TOC]; S29 ch. 16 (read: common failure patterns), ch. 14 (read: monitoring and alerting basics); S24; S25. **Proof:** brief part 3 — failure/security risk matrix in the section 3 failure-matrix template (delta: AI/pipeline dependencies), plus the validation plan with measurable targets, tests or experiments, and rollout/recovery criteria.

- **P10.8 — Design exercises: rate limiter, notification service, ledger/payment flow** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a, P10.5–P10.6. Three fixed scenarios rehearse the design method before the capstone brief review: for each, capacity numbers with assumptions, units, and uncertainty; one failure matrix in the section 3 failure-matrix template with a scenario-specific delta; and a reused-unit map naming which earlier units each decision draws on — P10.5's overload mechanisms and P10.6's distribution trade-offs are applied, never re-taught. **Proof:** one exercise packet with three checkable scenario sub-items, each judged against the P10.5/P10.6 criteria. Sources: S25; S27 ch. 28 (read); S19 ch. 7 (read); perf map L5–L6. Mentor synthesis: the three scenarios and their workload assumptions; the mechanisms come from the cited chapters, applied rather than re-taught.

- **P10.7 — Design review before implementation: go/adjust/stop** — **Priority:** important. Depends on: P10.4a–P10.4c, P10.3. A written adversarial review of the completed brief: does the whole design still answer the P2.1 Problem Card and P10.1 problem statement at the P10.4a cost assumptions; which brief items are weakest and why; which P7.5c risks got the cheapest validation; and what explicitly is NOT built in v1. The review outcome is the go/adjust/stop decision and the hand-off of the validation plan to Phase 14 — a new decision here reopens the specific P10.4 part rather than rewriting the brief. **Proof:** the review record naming at least three concrete weaknesses found (one per brief part) and the go/adjust/stop decision with reasons. Sources: S20 ch. 22; S33.
